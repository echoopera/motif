# Client partnership and creative practice
These client-partnership practices are active operating instructions in Design Harness 3.0. Use them proportionally; a minor change does not need a full presentation or expanded approval process.

## 1. Partnership ownership
Assign client-partnership accountability at intake. For a small task the coordinator can wear this role; for sustained work use the Client Partnership Lead. Maintain the client brief, including success, vocabulary, preferences, decision owners and constraints. The coordinator retains operational authority.

## 2. Shared care
Every role follows [client care](client-care.md). Evidence of care includes fewer repeated questions, clear decisions, accurate promises, completed details and recoverable errors. Do not measure quality through ingratiating language or assumed client emotions.

## 3. Feedback loop
Use [feedback and decisions](../harness/templates/feedback-decisions.md).
Capture exact feedback and context; classify it as observed failure, underlying need, aesthetic preference or proposed solution. Interpret tentatively. For a reversible, clear request, implement within scope without asking again. Clarify only material ambiguity.
Translate into a proposed change, relevant scenario, acceptance, impact and owner. Coordinator approves changes to task scope within its authority; obtain a client decision only where existing authorization is insufficient. Implement, review and record what changed or why it was not adopted. Conflicting stakeholder requests go to the recorded decision owner, not whichever speaker was last.

Example: “It feels cramped” may mean targets are too close, information is dense or hierarchy is weak. Inspect the actual task before changing every spacing token.

## 4. Creative ambition and craft
Brand direction records the intended character, useful differentiation, constraints and a few concrete reference attributes in the brief. When exploration is warranted, compare meaningfully different approaches to the same task. Recommend one; do not blend incompatible directions to avoid choosing.
Reserve a bounded craft pass in the existing budget. Review real text, optical alignment, type hierarchy, state transitions, motion, loading, empty/error copy, responsive content fit and accessibility. Name one product-appropriate opportunity for delight, such as clearer musical feedback or elegant document recovery. It is optional if it adds distraction, delay or scope.
Use [craft review](../harness/templates/craft-review.md). Design QA reviews artifacts and records defects separately from subjective preference. Craft work cannot reopen accepted strategy without a reason and an impact assessment.

## 5. Consultative presentation
Use [client review](../harness/templates/client-review.md). Lead with the desired outcome, demonstrate a representative user journey, explain the few decisions that matter, and give a recommendation. Show alternatives only when they aid a real choice.
Identify what is implemented, simulated and untested. Connect the work to the client's success without invented metrics. End with the specific decision required or a clear delivery status when no decision is needed. Honor concise and asynchronous review preferences.

## 6. Constructive dissent
At a meaningful review, ask: are we solving the right problem, following habit, decorating a weak workflow, or ignoring someone who needs to use it?
Record significant disagreement with scenario evidence, likely impact and an alternative. Brand direction resolves visual coherence; architecture resolves interfaces; coordinator resolves scope/priority; the client decides the authorized product tradeoff. Log the disposition. Do not silently override a client's preference; do not compromise required accessibility, security or truthfulness to win approval. Explain real conflicts and propose a workable route.

## 7. Commitments and recovery
The Engagement Lead alone writes [commitments](../harness/templates/commitments.md); other roles propose entries in owned handoffs. Each promise names outcome, owner, authorization, dependencies, target date or condition and status. Do not invent a date where none is agreed.
Before making a promise, validate capacity and dependency evidence. When risk appears, assess the impact before it becomes a surprise. If a commitment is missed: acknowledge the miss, name the consequence, own the remedy, offer feasible choices and record the resulting decision. No silent deadlines, automatic scope expansion or blaming another agent.
Commitment tracking is an artifact, not a reminder service. Schedule or contact only when explicitly authorized.

## 8. Aftercare
Integration and content design prepare [handoff and aftercare](../harness/templates/client-aftercare.md): a walkthrough, editable/source assets, run instructions, ownership, maintenance, limitations and recovery. The Engagement Lead checks that the client can understand what to do next.
Agree a bounded support period or next review only if requested/authorized; otherwise provide a proposed plan. Do not imply ongoing monitoring. Client enablement is verified through available reproducible steps; a real client walkthrough is marked unperformed until it happens.

## Integration with acceptance
At relevant reviews record whether client feedback is addressed, craft defects are resolved, commitments match delivery and handoff is usable. Review output is not accepted until the existing integration and baseline rules are satisfied. Internal checks should not create redundant permission requests. Missing feedback or an unperformed client walkthrough must be labeled honestly, not used to fabricate acceptance.
