01 / PRODUCT INTENT

A living musical landscape

Problem: navigating between complete recordings often produces abrupt timing changes, unstable pitch processing, competing harmony and an undifferentiated wall of sound. CONTINUUM makes the space between compositions playable and legible.

Primary user: an artist or curious listener who wants to explore ambient and electronic music through spatial gestures, without needing to program a sequencer. The first experience must expose the map and listening controls immediately.

Explore

Approach a node to hear its character. Leave it to hear the next composition emerge.

Compose

Draw an arbitrary route. Location, curvature, sphere size and harmonic relationships shape its interpretation.

Return

Save the route, constellation, audio references and musical settings. Replay or export the composition’s recipe.

Success: listeners understand where they are, which sources are audible, how the next transition will unfold and how to save a meaningful musical journey. Novel paths create variation; mathematical uniqueness or bit-identical replay is not promised.

02 / LISTENER JOURNEY

One map, four compositions

01 / ACTIVATEExplicitly initialize audio. No autoplay.
02 / POPULATEUse the synthesized field or import four recordings.
03 / SHAPEAdjust influence rings and draw a route.
04 / TRAVELFollow the beat-driven playhead and save a snapshot.

Pointer proximity provides immediate exploration. Touch selects nodes and draws directly. Keyboard arrows navigate; Space begins or ends a keyboard path. Play starts an audio-clock-driven journey, Pause preserves its position, and playback stops at the destination after travel ends.

New constellations randomize node positions without changing saved journeys. Influence radii remain adjustable during playback. JSON exports preserve a recipe; audio bytes remain in the browser and must be available for replay.

04 / TRANSITION + HARMONY

The handoff is a musical event

Transition policy

  1. Approach: incoming influence rises smoothly. Focused mode retains the strongest two sources to reduce four-track congestion.
  2. Lock: the outgoing musical field remains the reference during overlap. Imported tracks follow one shared tempo and tonic, using independent transport speed and pitch controls.
  3. Align: correct incoming beat position when inaudible; use bounded drift correction once audible. Phrase boundaries supply sync anchors.
  4. Blend: matched scale families allow a broad handoff. Harmony guard narrows incompatible scale overlaps and gently dips their combined level.
  5. Arrive: after one destination exceeds 88% influence, adopt its field on a bar boundary and ease toward its native tempo. Avoid chasing the nearest node on every frame.

Beat and tempo contract

The synthesized clock schedules ahead in audio time. Imported playback uses corrected first-beat anchors and integer-bar loop regions where source duration permits. Native and shared tempos may use musically compatible half/double-time relationships. Onset-derived beat anchors and filename BPM hints require user review; variable-tempo recordings are outside the current fixed-grid contract.

Pitch and scale contract

Short transpositions match tonics using the nearest semitone distance. Synthesized voices follow the shared scale directly. Imported full mixes retain their internal intervals; a major recording shifted to the same tonic as a minor recording remains major.

Required distinction: “scale-aware transition” means recognizing and managing an incompatibility. It does not mean the current player has rewritten every note in a polyphonic mix. True scale conversion requires source-aware partial/stem processing or symbolic reconstruction, plus listening validation.

Playback quality

The current player uses bounded waveform-aligned overlap/add, improving phase coherence over unaligned grain starts. Stereo channels share grain positions and timing. This remains a prototype time/pitch processor; transparent full-mix stretching, transient preservation and large transpositions need dedicated DSP evaluation. Existing storage is decoded at 24 kHz to bound memory.

05 / SPATIAL AUDIO DIRECTION

The listener moves; the sound field responds

Each source has a position, and the playhead acts as the listener. Horizontal separation produces restrained stereo direction; relative distance produces a subtle high-frequency change. A small HRTF contribution supplies headphone depth while retaining the original stereo recording as the main path.

No claim of Dolby Atmos output, Apple Spatial Audio integration, personalized HRTF, ambisonic export or device motion tracking is made by this version.

06 / PERFORMANCE RACK

Four effects. Twenty presets.

Each source has an independent 0–100% level before spatialization. The stereo master rack processes the combined field in this order: Granular → Delay → Reverb → Cutoff + LFO → output compressor. Five presets plus Bypass and a dry/wet control are available for every effect. All effects start bypassed for a new session.

ModuleFive presetsImplementation
GranularSoft cloud; Reverse mist; Prism swarm; Frozen constellation; Micro nebula.Live stereo capture, two-second ring, fixed twelve-grain pool, shared timing, reverse and pitched fragments, freeze.
DelayTape echo; Dotted eighth; Ping-pong; Dub spiral; Orbital echoes.Beat-synchronized stereo delay with filtered cross-feedback, bounded feedback and smoothed delay-time changes.
ReverbSmall room; Luminous plate; Cathedral; Gated bloom; Void chamber.Synthetic stereo convolution impulses, predelay, tone filtering and dual-engine preset crossfades. Impulses approximate these treatments; they are not sampled physical spaces.
Cutoff + LFOLowpass drift; Resonant tide; Bandpass orbit; Stepped transmission; Random beacon.Native filter, tempo-synchronized oscillator modulation and deterministic sample-and-hold cutoff changes.

Track levels, effect selections and wet amounts are included in working-session recovery and saved journey recipes. Frozen audio itself is transient and is not stored in JSON. Individual per-track effect racks, MIDI and automation recording remain outside this build.

Completion stops audio at the destination without resetting the initialized workspace. Replay resumes the context and the same route. Native browser suspension still requires a user gesture to resume sound.

07 / SCI-FI DESIGN SYSTEM

A cinematic instrument, not an ornamental dashboard

The supplied film-interface references guide the visual language: near-black command surfaces, fine cyan wireframes, segmented instrument rules, sparse pale-yellow accents, orbital geometry and precise telemetry. Four source identities stay legible without filling the map with unrelated readouts. Wireframe emitter spheres reveal source locations. Their outer rings reveal influence; contribution arcs and live waveforms expose actual signal; directional vectors reveal active contributors; a bright playhead anchors the journey. The first screen stays centered on listening and navigation.

Workspace composition

Desktop: large map and adjacent musical/transition/influence panels. Tablet and phone: map first, followed by influence, journey controls, telemetry and spatial controls; effect bays and source cards reflow below. Decorative motion must not compete with playhead feedback.

Inclusive interaction

  • Explicit audio activation, mute and visibility suspension.
  • Semantic node buttons, labeled range inputs and a keyboard path alternative.
  • Touch gestures with cancellation; no hover requirement on mobile.
  • Reduced motion preserves playhead position and removes decorative pulse.
  • Readable contrast and visible focus; status changes are not communicated by color alone.
  • Screen-reader and real-device conformance remain unverified until tested.

Performance targets for the next device pass: render control feedback at 60 Hz where feasible, keep scheduling independent of rendering, and bound processing/memory on representative iPhone and desktop hardware. These are targets, not measured latency claims.

Known prototype limits include heuristic key/tempo estimates, fixed-tempo and 4/4 assumptions, 24 kHz preview decoding, browser storage quotas, memory cost for four long recordings and audible artifacts under large stretch/pitch changes. The current importer accepts clips up to 8 minutes / 160 MB each.
